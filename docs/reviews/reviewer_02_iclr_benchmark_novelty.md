# Reviewer 02 — ICLR (benchmark + agent: novelty and positioning)

## Summary of the submission as I understand it
The paper introduces a benchmark for the digital reunion of dispersed cultural-heritage ensembles (altarpiece panels, album leaves, manuscript fragments now in different institutions), built from Wikidata, Commons, NGA open data and Wikipedia, with a K=30 shortlist per query and 80 dev / 294 test queries. An "LLM reunion agent" selects the ensemble members from the shortlist; a seven-rung information ladder plus closed-book, counterfactual forced-choice, holder/creator/title/dimension ablations and shortlist-shortcut controls separate evidence use from memorisation, across roughly ten model families, with pair-verification, visual-gate, pointwise and deep-research side experiments. I have the run ledger but no accuracy numbers, so I assess what is claimed and how it is instrumented, not whether it works.

## Scores (1–10) with one-line justification each
- Novelty: 5 — the task and leakage-aware protocol are new to ML venues; the selector is listwise LLM reranking under another name and the memorisation probe belongs to a known family.
- Technical soundness: 6 — the right controls; the "agent" label and the nestedness of the ladder are overstated.
- Evaluation rigour: 5 — shortcut baselines live in follow-up scripts, not the main table; test coverage below L1 is thin.
- Clarity (of the design, as reconstructed): 6 — the ladder is legible from the flags; the retriever, the metric and what makes the method an agent are not.
- Impact / significance: 7 — a consequential, non-synthetic set-membership task where contamination is the central issue; durable if versioned.
- Overall: 5 — 7 with items 1, 3, 6 and 7 below.

## Strengths
- A real decision problem with a crisp unit (ensemble membership) and asymmetric cost: a false reunion pollutes scholarship, so abstention matters.
- Memorisation is treated as the primary confound from the start: closed-book baseline, both-orders forced choice with creator masked and evidence withheld, holder scrub, title withholding, dimension permutation, temporal ladder.
- `e10_shortlist_controls.py` and the `shortlist_shortcuts` report acknowledge the hypothesis-only-baseline problem.
- Leaderboard-grade bookkeeping: served-model audit, tokens, cost and latency per run.

## Weaknesses
1. **MAJOR — the "agent" is one listwise call.** Every E10 run has exactly one call per query (80/80 dev, 294/294 test) and 40–110 completion tokens on non-thinking models — a pick list; no tools, retrieval or verification loop. The genuinely agentic systems are side experiments: E15 (295 usage records for 40 queries, ≈7 steps/query, GPT-4.1-mini only) and E17 (211 records for 20 queries, GPT-5.4 only); the visual gate (E10c) is scored once and replayed over L1/L3/L5 runs — a rung-independent post-hoc filter. Rename the main method a listwise selector, or make the agent the headline with the agent's numbers.
2. **MAJOR — positioning against LLM re-ranking is missing.** Selecting members from a K=30 shortlist is listwise reranking (RankGPT/LRL lineage) with a set-valued output; E6 `t1_rerank` already *is* that baseline but ran on 40 queries of one model. What is genuinely new is the set-cardinality decision (0…K members, abstention allowed) — say so, and add a retriever-only baseline (top-m by retriever score, m tuned on dev) plus a shuffled-shortlist run: position bias in listwise LLM reranking is documented, and the retriever's order may be doing the work.
3. **MAJOR — the ladder is an ablation until it is a schema.** Rungs are not nested: L2-T shows *more* prompt tokens than L1 (2,316 vs 1,959), and `--inputs v2` (3,391 tok) is a parallel ladder run on one model. It becomes a contribution only if (a) the field set per rung is published as a versioned schema, (b) each rung maps to a real catalogue state (L5 ≈ auction-stub metadata, L6 ≈ unlabelled fragment), and (c) rung degradation is validated per query against an independent memorisation measure.
4. **MAJOR — the counterfactual forced choice is a known construction.** Forced choice between the true item and a perturbed alternative, both orders, is the design of the Data Contamination Quiz (Golchin & Surdeanu) and of name-cloze probes (Chang et al., 2023); counterfactual task variants are Wu et al.'s "Reasoning or Reciting?". Discuss Min-K%/perplexity-gap membership inference, guided prompting and exchangeability tests (Oren et al.) and why they do not apply (no logprobs from reasoning models; no canonical order). What *is* new is the target: with `--withhold-evidence --mask-creator` the probe measures associative memory of a fact (X belongs to ensemble Y), not memorisation of benchmark text. The former does not prove the test set was seen; the latter is what readers will assume. Say which you detect.
5. **MAJOR — the benchmark can be gamed through the shortlist.** (a) *Different holder*: dispersed ensembles are cross-institution by definition, so "holder differs from the query" is a shortcut; L3-H removes the field but not holder-specific record formatting (NGA vs Wikidata styles — `--balance-source` in E7 shows the authors know). (b) *Same creator / date window*: if the retriever blocks on creator, selecting every same-creator candidate may saturate recall. (c) *Title stems*: 32/80 dev and 91/294 test titles name the ensemble, so 31–40 % of L1 is near-free. (d) *Dimension consistency* is legitimate, but a fixed-tolerance rule may already capture it. Each shortcut should be a leaderboard row at every rung, with "LLM minus best shortcut" reported.
6. **MINOR — ground-truth provenance.** If membership comes from Wikidata `part of` statements, every label is in every pretraining corpus and every Wikidata error is a benchmark error; `quality_audit` / `release_validation` should quantify a human-verified subset.

## Experiments or artefacts that would change my score
1. Retriever-only and the four shortcut heuristics as rows of the main table at every rung. *Convince me:* LLM beats the best shortcut by more than the CI at L5 and L6.
2. Shuffled vs retriever-ordered shortlist at L1 and L5 for two models (≈$0.3–1.6 per run). *Convince me:* < 3 pp change.
3. A per-query cross-tab: closed-book-known (E5) × forced-choice-memorised (E8) × correct at L6. *Convince me:* non-trivial accuracy in the "unknown and unmemorised" cell — the evidence-driven claim.
4. The deep-research harness on the full dev set for one model, with a steps-vs-accuracy-vs-cost curve. *Convince me:* a regime where agency beats the single call at ≤3× cost; otherwise drop "agent".
5. A released rung schema (JSON field lists), the regate rule and the retriever spec under a version tag.
6. A temporal slice: ensembles whose membership statement entered Wikidata after each model's cutoff (the `temporal_ladder` report suggests the data exists). *Convince me:* post-cutoff L1 accuracy within CI of pre-cutoff; if not, the framing should flip to "memorisation dominates".
7. Durability: public dev, canary-tagged public test, a sealed private test of equal size with yearly refresh, and inter-annotator agreement on a 50-query verified subset. *Convince me:* the private/public gap is reported in v1.

## Token-efficiency / cost critique
- **Wasteful.** GPT-5.4-mini costs as much per call as GPT-5.4 ($0.016–0.023 vs $0.013–0.020), is 3× slower on test (14–15 s/call) and its 450–770 completion tokens are hidden reasoning nobody can audit — for a leaderboard, report cost-per-correct-reunion, not accuracy alone. E17 at $0.22 and 25k tokens per query is 11× the shortlist agent on the same model; at n=20 it is an anecdote. E16: $12.65 (12.6 % of budget) on dev-only re-scoring of a Qwen3-VL-2B shortlist.
- **Cheap and missing.** Retriever-only and shortcut rows: $0. E6 baseline from 40 to 80 queries: $0.22. Shuffled-order run: $0.33 on GPT-4.1-mini. Closed-book on test: ≤ $2.2 per model. E8 on GPT-5.4 / GPT-5.4-mini: ≈ $2.4 each.
- **Prompt design.** A cheap-filter-then-verify cascade (GPT-4.1-mini prunes 30 → 8, flagship decides) would cut flagship prompt tokens ≈4×; E16's `--k 8` does this for images, not for text, where the money is.

## One idea the authors have not tried
Turn the ladder into an online measurement: start every query at L6 and give the model a tool `reveal(candidate_id, field)` with a per-query budget (e.g. 10 reveals), where fields are exactly the rung increments already defined (title, holder, creator, dimensions, free text). Score accuracy as a function of reveals and log which fields are requested first: a model asking for titles and holders is seeking names to match against memory; one asking for dimensions and medium is reasoning from physical evidence. This makes the method an actual agent, converts seven offline conditions into one information-value curve per model, and yields a per-query memorisation signal that needs no logprobs, so it works for reasoning models. Cost is E15-like (≈8k tokens/query): ≈$0.6 for 80 dev queries on GPT-4.1-mini, ≈$3 on GPT-5.4.

## Confidence (1–5) and what I could not assess
4. I could not assess the retriever, the metric, the ground-truth source, the rung schema, the related-work section, or any accuracy or shortcut-baseline numbers; the "one call per query" and coverage observations come directly from `runs.csv`.
